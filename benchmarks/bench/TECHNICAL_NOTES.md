# Bench scene: viewer rendering and relighting benchmark

Measured on 28 October 2026 (local time). These are measured release-build results, not estimates.

## Paper-ready result

On an Apple M1 Pro (14 GPU cores, 16 GB unified memory), the desktop interval viewer rendered the Bench scene (650,000 vertices, 4,207,572 tetrahedra) at **18.84 FPS at 1280×720** and **17.48 FPS at 1920×1080**, using the High quality preset. Mean synchronized end-to-end frame latency was **53.08 ms** and **57.21 ms**, respectively. A complete single-point-light transport recomputation took **1.344 s** at 720p and **1.345 s** at 1080p. When only that light's intensity changed, cached transport recombination took **4.16 ms** and **4.30 ms**. Relighting times measure isolated bake completion, not concurrent rendering or time until an updated frame is displayed.

## Measurements

| Resolution | Operation | N | Mean (ms) | SD (ms) | Median (ms) | P95 (ms) | Rate (s⁻¹) |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1280×720 | render | 300 | 53.08 | 3.14 | 53.01 | 57.42 | 18.84 |
| 1280×720 | relight_full | 10 | 1343.54 | 9.34 | 1344.07 | 1361.86 | 0.74 |
| 1280×720 | relight_cached | 10 | 4.16 | 0.10 | 4.17 | 4.28 | 240.30 |
| 1920×1080 | render | 300 | 57.21 | 2.40 | 57.41 | 60.93 | 17.48 |
| 1920×1080 | relight_full | 10 | 1345.29 | 6.38 | 1345.46 | 1357.71 | 0.74 |
| 1920×1080 | relight_cached | 10 | 4.30 | 0.14 | 4.34 | 4.46 | 232.75 |

SD is sample standard deviation. P95 uses the nearest-rank definition; with ten bake samples it is the maximum observed sample. Rate is 1000 divided by mean milliseconds: FPS for rendering, completed isolated updates per second for relighting. The 300 frames and ten updates are consecutive measurements within one process per resolution, not independent repeated launches or a confidence interval.

## Method

- Actual desktop viewer, release build, wgpu 28.0.0 / Metal, macOS 26.6.2 (25G83), Rust 1.94.0. Hardware: MacBookPro18,3, M1 Pro with 8 CPU cores and 14 GPU cores, 16 GB memory.
- Asset: `crates/rmesh-viewer-web/web/bench.rmesh` (local symlink to `/Users/amai/Downloads/bench.rmesh`). SHA-256: `541874516ca7e74cc3496b617491ccf29ebf15f5d4052b18893d19be95ac1608`.
- Bench asset: diffuse/PBR materials, SH degree 0.
- Fixed default viewer orbit camera: initial position approximately `(0.109944, -3.387943, 0.483478)`, looking toward the origin with 50-degree vertical FOV. The viewer initializes its orbit camera from the asset position, rather than applying the stored quaternion. No camera input or animation was introduced.
- One default warm point light, generated by `light_bake::default_light` from the scene's start position. Zero ambient. Default opaque primitives and egui remain part of the viewer rendering workload.
- High preset: GPU 32-bit depth sorting, compute interval generation (`RMESH_MESH=0`), shell and Hi-Z enabled, importance threshold 0.01. The 1080p log records 342,188 shell boundary faces and 3,382,659 tetrahedra retained by importance filtering (before other visibility tests). These are approximate interactive settings, not Reference quality.
- `RMESH_WIN` requests physical pixel dimensions; the CSV records actual render-surface dimensions, verified as 1280×720 and 1920×1080. Vsync and user input are disabled by benchmark mode.
- Finish the initial lighting bake, discard 60 ready frames, then measure 300 frames. Each sample starts immediately before `App::render` and stops after `device.poll(wait_indefinitely())` confirms GPU completion. This includes CPU update/encoding, rendering, profiling/readback housekeeping, egui, surface acquisition/presentation and synchronization. Asset loading, pipeline creation, initial bake, event-loop gaps and warm-up are excluded. These are serialized end-to-end latencies, not isolated GPU timestamp durations or unconstrained pipelined throughput.
- After rendering, alternate the point light between X offsets of +0.1 and −0.1 scene units from its original position and explicitly invalidate transport. Run eleven full bakes, discard the first and retain ten. Each bake has 40 dispatch batches of at most 16,384 vertices, including final radiance combination. Submit and wait for GPU completion after every batch. No frames are rendered during these isolated measurements.
- For cached updates, retain the last light position and alternately multiply intensity by 1.1 and 1/1.1. Again discard the first update and retain ten. Each update takes one batch and reuses the single-light transport cache. It does not retrace visibility.

## Interpretation and limits

1080p has 2.25 times as many pixels, but measured frame latency is only 1.078 times the 720p latency in this fixed view. This is consistent with substantial resolution-independent geometry, CPU, and synchronization costs; these measurements alone do not identify the bottleneck. A different viewpoint, quality setting or visible-tet count can change performance significantly.

Full bake work is vertex/light dependent rather than screen-resolution dependent, explaining its similar cost at both resolutions. Cached intensity recombination is roughly 313–323 times faster than full transport recomputation here. That advantage applies to the single-light cached edit tested; moving a light requires full transport work.

The interactive viewer spreads a full bake over frames and retains the previous completed lighting meanwhile. The isolated 1.34-second measurement is therefore not interactive light-to-display latency: that also depends on rendering, frame scheduling and the final presentation. Initial loading/bake time is also excluded. The existing informational baker log is not the measurement source; it can mark CPU-side readiness before final GPU completion and can retain its previous start time on recolor. Use the synchronized CSV durations.

Only one machine, one scene and one fixed camera were evaluated. No thermal/power controls or independent process repetitions were performed. The performance run itself did not measure image error; the High-versus-Reference comparison below separately quantifies preset differences. The asset produces warnings about 2,134 non-manifold faces with three or more claimants, which the loader leaves unmatched; the raw logs preserve these warnings.

The source checkout contained substantial pre-existing modifications. Base Git commit: `e8d3b8109ffadf540882434d0f43c1a91c615432`; that commit alone does not reproduce this working-tree renderer. Preserve this working tree or commit it before treating these as final archival paper results.

## Reproduce

Run from the repository root. The asset must be available locally. The viewer opens a window and exits after all samples are written. Output directories must already exist. Avoid interaction, other GPU workloads and overrides such as screenshots/oracle mode when benchmarking.

```sh
cargo build --release -p rmesh-viewer
mkdir -p benchmarks/bench
RUST_LOG=warn RMESH_BENCHMARK=benchmarks/bench/720p.csv RMESH_WIN=1280x720 RMESH_QUALITY=High RMESH_MESH=0 target/release/rmesh-viewer crates/rmesh-viewer-web/web/bench.rmesh > benchmarks/bench/720p.log 2>&1
RUST_LOG=info RMESH_BENCHMARK=benchmarks/bench/1080p.csv RMESH_WIN=1920x1080 RMESH_QUALITY=High RMESH_MESH=0 target/release/rmesh-viewer crates/rmesh-viewer-web/web/bench.rmesh > benchmarks/bench/1080p.log 2>&1
python3 scripts/summarize_viewer_benchmark.py benchmarks/bench/720p.csv benchmarks/bench/1080p.csv
```

The log levels above match the recorded runs; the 720p process inherited `RUST_LOG=warn`, whereas the 1080p process recorded informational setup details. Logging remains within timed work where emitted.

Raw data: [720p CSV](720p.csv), [1080p CSV](1080p.csv). Logs: [720p](720p.log), [1080p](1080p.log). Each CSV contains operation, actual width/height, sample index, elapsed milliseconds and bake batch count. The summary script rejects incomplete sample sets, non-positive/non-finite durations and changing resolutions.

## Feature and validation

Set `RMESH_BENCHMARK=<output.csv>` to benchmark any supported viewer scene with a light. The mode disables vsync/input, waits for initial lighting, warms up, records samples, performs full and cached relighting measurements, writes CSV and exits. Cached updates reuse transport for a single light; multiple lights may require a full bake.

Validation: release build succeeded; both GPU runs completed; the summarizer verified 300 rendering samples, ten full relights and ten cached updates at each requested resolution. Full bakes consistently used 40 batches and cached updates one batch. No renderer/shader behavior was changed by this feature.

## Image error: High versus Reference preset

Using the same Bench scene, fixed camera and default point light, High has full-image linear-RGB **MSE 1.651×10⁻⁵ at 720p** and **1.656×10⁻⁵ at 1080p** against the viewer's Reference preset. Both presets use the same diffuse lighting bake and GPU 32-bit sort. High enables the density shell and importance culling (threshold 0.01); Reference disables both. Other visibility settings remain at the same viewer defaults.

| Resolution | Linear RGB MSE | RMSE | Mean absolute error | Maximum absolute error |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1280×720 | 1.6509764e-05 | 0.0040632208 | 0.002543524 | 0.088500977 |
| 1920×1080 | 1.6555044e-05 | 0.004068789 | 0.0025448648 | 0.10046387 |

MSE = sum((High − Reference)²) / (width × height × 3), evaluated in float64 over every RGB value, including background. Images are pre-tone-map linear HDR RGB captured from the viewer's float16 color attachment into float32 NumPy arrays. There is no clipping, gamma conversion, alignment, masking, or exposure normalization. Opaque helpers/light gizmos are hidden for these scene-only captures, and egui is not included in the linear color attachment. This differs from the end-to-end timing workload, which includes UI and default primitives.

Each image was captured at frame 120 or later, after lighting was ready for consecutive frames. The comparison script verifies identical actual dimensions, intrinsics, camera position/orientation, near/far planes, light position/color/intensity, and bake mode in the capture sidecars. It also verifies that shell and importance are enabled only in High. This comparison measures the selected preset approximations; Reference here means the viewer's Reference preset with the same diffuse bake.

The maximum channel errors are 0.08850 and 0.10046. Thus the small mean squared errors do not imply a per-pixel absolute error bound of 0.01. At that threshold, 45,027/921,600 pixels (4.89%) at 720p and 101,710/2,073,600 pixels (4.90%) at 1080p exceed the threshold in at least one channel.

Machine-readable results, including per-channel MSE: [accuracy/metrics.json](accuracy/metrics.json). Raw captures: [High 720p](accuracy/high-720p.npy), [Reference 720p](accuracy/reference-720p.npy), [High 1080p](accuracy/high-1080p.npy), [Reference 1080p](accuracy/reference-1080p.npy). Each has a `.npy.json` camera/light sidecar. Corresponding PNG files are display screenshots, not the metric inputs. [Capture manifest](accuracy/manifest.json) records asset, executable, and image SHA-256 hashes.

Reproduce the four captures and summary from the repository root:

```sh
mkdir -p benchmarks/bench/accuracy
for size in 1280x720 1920x1080; do
  case "$size" in 1280x720) label=720p ;; 1920x1080) label=1080p ;; esac
  for preset in High Reference; do
    case "$preset" in High) stem=high ;; Reference) stem=reference ;; esac
    output="benchmarks/bench/accuracy/$stem-$label"
    RUST_LOG=warn RMESH_QUALITY="$preset" RMESH_MESH=0 RMESH_VSYNC=0 \
      RMESH_WIN="$size" RMESH_SHOT_FRAME=120 RMESH_SHOT="$output.png" \
      RMESH_LINEAR_SHOT="$output.npy" target/release/rmesh-viewer \
      crates/rmesh-viewer-web/web/bench.rmesh > "$output.log" 2>&1
  done
done
python3 scripts/summarize_viewer_accuracy.py benchmarks/bench/accuracy
```

Validation: all four GPU captures completed at the requested physical resolutions; camera/light metadata matched; all five comparison-unit tests passed, including an analytical MSE case with values above one to verify that HDR differences are not clipped.
